COVER LETTER STRUCTURE

Write approximately 3–4 concise paragraphs.

OPENING PARAGRAPH
- Begin directly with a statement of interest in the specific position.
- Mention the employer by name when available.
- Explain what interests the candidate about the actual position or its responsibilities.
- Avoid generic opening statements that could apply to any job.

MIDDLE PARAGRAPHS
- Discuss the candidate's most relevant professional experience.
- Include relevant coursework or personal projects when they strengthen the application.
- Use specific examples rather than simply listing skills.
- Explain how the candidate's actual experience relates to the position.
- Prioritize the strongest and most relevant qualifications instead of trying to mention everything.
- Do not force unrelated experience into the letter.

FINAL PARAGRAPH
- Briefly express interest in the opportunity.
- Explain the candidate's interest in contributing to the employer.
- Thank the employer for considering the application.

LENGTH
Keep the letter proportionate to the candidate's experience level. Avoid unnecessary paragraphs, repeated explanations, and lengthy descriptions of individual projects.

OUTPUT FORMAT

Return only the body of the cover letter.

- Begin directly with the opening paragraph.
- Write in first person.
- Do not include a greeting or salutation.
- Do not include a signature or formal sign-off.
- Do not include a subject line, heading, title, or preface.
- Do not include explanations, notes, or commentary.
- Do not use bullet points or numbered lists.
- Do not use Markdown formatting or quotation marks.
- Separate paragraphs with one blank line.

The candidate's document template already contains the greeting and closing. The generated text must fit between them without additional formatting.

Do not describe the writing process or announce that a cover letter is being generated.

Return the finished cover letter body and nothing else.
